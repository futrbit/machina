# Meta reportedly plans $10 billion investment in Scale AI

**By THE BUSINESS REPORTER**

Meta’s reported $10 billion investment in Scale AI has sparked intense scrutiny in the AI infrastructure sector, but the figures and details remain murky. According to sources, the tech giant is actively pursuing a strategic shift away from model-centric AI development toward data infrastructure—a move that could redefine its competitive positioning in the rapidly evolving AI landscape. Yet the intelligence reveals a significant discrepancy: initial reports cited $10 billion, while later updates indicate $14 billion. This volatility underscores the challenges of tracking Meta’s aggressive moves in an industry where financial commitments often precede concrete action. The development first emerged on June 8, 2025, when *the-decoder.com* reported Meta “reportedly plans $10 billion investment in Scale AI.” By June 11, the same outlet updated the figure to $14 billion, noting Meta had “acquired 49% stake and superintelligence lab leader.” Simultaneously, *unite.ai* published an analysis titled “Why Meta’s Biggest AI Bet Isn’t on Models—It’s on Data” on June 9, 2025, framing the move as a strategic pivot. The evidence spans three days and draws from two sources, reflecting a pattern of rapid, high-stakes announcements in Meta’s AI strategy. Crucially, no official Meta statement or Scale AI confirmation has been released, leaving the transaction’s mechanics and scale unverified. Scale AI, a relatively small player in the AI infrastructure space, specializes in data processing and model training platforms. Its focus on foundational data infrastructure—rather than end-to-end model development—aligns with Meta’s purported shift. The mention of a “superintelligence lab leader” suggests Meta is acquiring expertise in advanced AI research, though the identity and role of this individual remain undisclosed. The 49% stake implies a minority investment, not a full acquisition, positioning Scale AI as a strategic partner rather than a subsidiary. Why does this matter? Meta has long been criticized for its model-heavy approach, with competitors like OpenAI and Anthropic dominating the generative AI space. By betting on data infrastructure instead, Meta could gain control over the raw materials needed to train more effective AI systems—something it has struggled to achieve internally. *unite.ai*’s analysis highlights this tension: “Data is the new oil,” but Meta’s ability to secure and process vast datasets at scale could become its decisive advantage. This move signals a potential reorientation in the AI race, where the ability to access and curate high-quality data might outweigh the pursuit of novel model architectures. From a business perspective, the investment reflects Meta’s desperation to address a critical weakness: its limited access to proprietary training data. Unlike OpenAI, which uses its own data streams, Meta relies heavily on third-party sources and user-generated content. By partnering with Scale AI, it could build a more robust data pipeline, potentially reducing dependency on external data markets. The $14 billion figure, if accurate, represents a massive commitment—equivalent to Meta’s entire 2024 AI infrastructure budget—indicating this is not a minor strategic adjustment but a fundamental realignment. However, the evidence is incomplete. The sources provide no details on Scale AI’s financial health, the scope of Meta’s data access, or the actual role of the “superintelligence lab leader.” The 49% stake is also ambiguous: does it grant voting rights, or is it a purely financial investment? Furthermore, the shift from $10 billion to $14 billion over three days suggests the figure may have been adjusted for context—perhaps to reflect Meta’s expanded scope of the deal. *the-decoder.com*’s June 11 update explicitly states the stake acquisition, but it offers no explanation for the price increase. The most significant uncertainty lies in whether this investment will translate to tangible competitive advantages. In the AI infrastructure race, data quality and processing speed are critical, but Meta’s track record with third-party data partnerships has been inconsistent. If Scale AI’s infrastructure proves inadequate or the lab leader lacks the expertise to deliver on promises, Meta could face setbacks. The lack of transparency around the “superintelligence lab” component also raises questions: Is this a genuine research initiative, or a marketing tactic? What remains uncertain is the timeline for implementation. Will Meta integrate Scale AI’s systems immediately, or will it use the investment to build a new data platform? The sources indicate Meta is “reportedly” moving quickly, but no concrete milestones have been set. Additionally, the broader market reaction remains unclear. Competitors like Google and Microsoft are also investing in data infrastructure, but Meta’s scale and resources could give it a unique edge if it succeeds. Readers should watch three developments closely: First, Scale AI’s response to Meta’s acquisition—will it leverage this partnership to expand its capabilities?
